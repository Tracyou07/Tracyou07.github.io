§ 03 Research Three labs, in reverse chronology — the working notebook entries.

GRASP Lab · Department of Robotics

Graduate Research Assistant · University of Pennsylvania
Philadelphia, USA
Oct 2025 — present
¶
3D Reconstruction & World-Model Prediction
  • Investigating single-view and multi-view 3D reconstruction pipelines; benchmarking depth-based (DPT) and correspondence-based (DUST3R, CUT3R, Human3R) methods for consistent geometry from monocular images.
  • Reproducing modern 3D point-correspondence methods to construct dense, temporally stable point clouds for downstream world-model prediction.
  • Building a world-model-style latent dynamics module to maintain temporal consistency across frames and reduce artefacts — mesh-body interpenetration, foot-sliding, geometry drift.
  • Exploring geometry-aware loss functions (Chamfer distance, normal consistency, contact constraints) to enforce physical plausibility in human–environment interactions.

De Artificial Intelligence Lab

Undergraduate Research Assistant
Chengdu, China
Apr 2024 — Nov 2025
¶
i. Training-Free Decoding for Hallucination Mitigation in LVLMs
  • Systematically analysed mainstream decoding strategies (DoLa, VCD, DAMO, SPIN) on LLaVA, mPLUG-Owl2 and InstructBLIP.
  • DCLA — an inter-layer decoding mechanism aggregating cross-layer hidden states to construct dynamic semantic references, stabilising semantic flow.
  • MAP — a 2-D semantic-map interpretation of hidden states with Layer-Wise Criss-Cross Attention and Global–Local Logit Fusion to gather factual cues across layers.
  • State-of-the-art hallucination suppression on MME, POPE and MMHal-Bench across multiple 7B LVLMs.
¶
ii. Token Efficiency & Visual Token Engineering
  • STAR — dual-phase token reduction: early-stage pruning via visual self-attention, late-stage via cross-modal attention. 95% token reduction, 58% FLOPs reduction, 101.3% performance.
  • Integrated STAR into LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, mPLUG-Owl2 and Qwen-VL — outperforming FastV, FasterVLM and SparseVLM baselines.
  • SeeMe — the first training-free hallucination mitigation framework via visual token engineering: Cross-Modal Attention Selection → Semantic Token Merging → Attention-Based Refinement.
